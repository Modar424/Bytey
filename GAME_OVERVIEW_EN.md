# BYTEWISE (Arabic title: «بايت وذكاء»)

> **Think Smart. Code Smarter.**
> *(Arabic tagline: "Test your mind, discover your mistakes, and level up!")*

A mobile (Android) educational quiz game about **programming, artificial intelligence and software engineering**. A little dinosaur called **Bytey** keeps you company, cheers you on and teases you gently. The game is available in Arabic and English and uses a dark theme only.

---

## What is the goal of the game?

To move you from "I know the terms" to **"I understand why, and I know how to choose."**

The game does not only test memorisation. It trains the way a developer thinks: how to work with an AI model without trusting it blindly, how to estimate the cost of a request, and how to make a real architecture decision and justify it. After every question you learn **why** an answer is right or wrong, not just whether it is.

---

## How do you play?

- **10 levels** of increasing difficulty, each with **30 questions**. You pass with **18 correct answers** to unlock the next level.
- **Six categories** in every level, 5 questions from each:
  1. General knowledge (history of programming, origins of names)
  2. Tokens and prompt engineering
  3. AI tools
  4. Models and performance
  5. Software architecture
  6. **Real-world scenarios** (complete design decisions)
- **You choose the time before every level:** from 20 to 90 seconds per question. It stays the same for the whole level.
- **Points reward speed:** a fast correct answer earns 15 points, a medium one 10, a slow one 5. After every 5 correct answers in a row, the next question is worth **double**.
- **You can go back to the previous question** and change your answer. You cannot exploit this to gain extra points: each question counts once, and its time is not reset by navigating.
- **After each answer** a short explanation tells you the reason.
- You can **leave in the middle of a level** and continue later from the same place.

## What makes it different?

> The comparison below is with the **common pattern** of educational quiz games, not with any specific game.

### 1. Questions that test understanding, not memory
Many quiz apps stop at definitions and facts. Here, a whole category is made of real-world scenarios, for example:
- *"A hospital wants to build a system for patient records and appointments. Which backend and database are the most sensible, and why?"*
- *"How do you stop an AI assistant from revealing a patient's data to someone who must not see it?"*
- *"After a short outage, retries overloaded the database. What is the fix?"*

Common mistakes are also material for questions: hallucinations, verifying AI output, prompt injection, and the token cost of Arabic text.

### 2. Current and practical content
It covers what developers face today: tokens, context windows, time to first token, model routing and retrieval-augmented generation, alongside system fundamentals such as caching, transactions, databases and scalability.

### 3. Convincing wrong answers
The options are written so that the correct one cannot be spotted by its length or shape, so you actually have to think.

### 4. Fair and flexible play
You pick the time before each level and can go back to the previous question, with protection against cheating: every question scores only once, and its clock is never refilled by moving around.

### 5. You learn from your mistakes
The **learning report** analyses your answers and identifies the concepts you are weak in, then suggests **learning resources and a practical challenge** for each one. The game does not end with a score; it points you to what to study next.

### 6. Bytey is a living character
- **He reacts to you:** tap him and he hops, makes a sound and answers back.
- **Spoken, sarcastic comments:** he comments on wrong answers, on going back to a previous question, and on leaving the game, and he hypes you up during winning streaks.
- **He evolves with you:** as you unlock more levels, his look changes (programmer glasses, headphones and more).

### 7. Real Arabic, not a cosmetic translation
A complete interface in Arabic (right-to-left) and English, and every question, option and explanation is available in both languages.

### 8. A calm, fast design
A dark interface, light animations, and questions and explanations stored inside the app itself, so you do not need the internet to play (resource links open in the browser when you want them). Your progress and settings are saved automatically.

---

## Who is it for?

Students, graduates and beginners in programming; developers who want to strengthen their understanding of AI and design decisions; and anyone who enjoys learning through challenge and fun.
